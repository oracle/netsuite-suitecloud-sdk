# Resources, assignment and scheduling

Use the recipe that fits the request. A work review, skill search, availability assessment or proposed schedule is read-only. Follow [SafeWords](../RUNBOOK.md#safewords) for authorization and retrieved content. Use [scheduling data](../references/scheduling-data.md) for relevant reads and their limits, and [scheduling](../references/scheduling.md) for FSM behavior. [Scheduling patterns](scheduling-patterns.md) offers examples. Follow the user's direct choices within the authorized scope.

## Review work to schedule

Establish the business scope, requested statuses, date basis and period from the question. Use supplied data or scoped account reads. Planned FSM appointments, native Start Date, Due Date and undated work answer different questions; do not substitute one for another. A generic review does not imply a review of today. For “this week,” use an established customer convention; otherwise state the Monday-through-Sunday local window used. Include appointments crossing the window boundary when the requested basis is appointment overlap.

Keep the identities of every task in each selected population, including tasks whose details cannot be read. Distinguish incomplete population coverage from enrichment gaps. Include a truly undated backlog when it matters to an unscheduled-work review or planning request: verify that both planned appointment endpoints are absent rather than treating an unread or one-sided schedule as undated. Keep Due Date and native dates as separate facts. Preserve assigned, In Progress or completed work when it meets the user's requested scope, even if it is not eligible for a new assignment. A work review does not assign anyone.

Report the observed population, scope, date basis, consequential gaps and task links where available. Do not infer a complete account-wide list from a partial or narrower search. See [scheduling data](../references/scheduling-data.md) for choosing and interpreting scoped populations.

## Find people with relevant skills

Establish the requested skills or the task's recorded requirements and the resource scope. Compare each person's complete recorded skill membership with the requirements. A request for recorded matches needs no appointment window, work-list population or calendar read. Requiring one person to hold every task skill is a rule only when an established customer practice or a selected pattern calls for it; do not pool or impose that rule by default.

Describe recorded matches separately from active status, Mobile User settings, Region or other eligibility, certification or skill expiry, proficiency, and availability. Missing qualification detail leaves that detail unknown; recorded membership alone does not establish it. If the user asks who can take the work, continue through availability and planning rather than treating a skill match as an assignment answer.

## Check availability and fit

Identify the interval and resources involved, including a proposed primary and any retained team members. Use applicable working calendars, dated holidays and exceptions, employee absences, primary and team task commitments, relevant Project Task work, and potentially continuing work. Check commitments around the interval when travel, breaks, work that started earlier or the next appointment could affect the answer. Interpret leave status and effective calendar selection under [scheduling](../references/scheduling.md); use [scheduling data](../references/scheduling-data.md) for the account reads and representation limits.

Assess the travel needed for the requested interval: from the known starting location, between jobs and onward to existing commitments. Use available travel estimates, disclose the estimates and their uncertainty, and allow sufficient travel time. Preserve existing breaks and use known shift arrangements and meal preferences. Resolve missing inputs when they materially affect feasibility; do not invent travel speeds, meal times or break durations. A short appointment does not automatically require a full-day itinerary audit. A buffer alone does not establish travel time, and last-known coordinates do not establish a starting point.

Keep user- or dispatcher-reported unavailable intervals separate from NetSuite absence records. Apply them as supplied constraints within their stated scope, and preserve their source when it affects the conclusion. An empty account-absence search does not cancel a reported constraint, and a reported constraint does not establish that an absence record exists. Do not create absence records from these reports.

Assess each source for the period needed by the claim. An interval can support an overlap finding; a daily capacity claim needs coverage of the full relevant local day. Recorded working hours can inform that review. Apply a numerical daily limit only when the user selects it or it is established customer practice. Preserve a known conflict even if another source is incomplete. Distinguish a verified clear source, no conflict observed in the available data, and supported availability across the adopted sources. If a check is missing, a useful conditional suggestion may still be possible: name the missing check and do not present the person as confirmed available. Incomplete evidence does not prove that the work cannot be scheduled.

## Build or revise a proposed schedule

Use the selected work, planning horizon, priorities, fixed appointments, flexible windows, customer practices and any supplied constraints. Ask only for a choice that materially changes the outcome and cannot be inferred from context. When a fixed appointment is selected, preserve its time unless the user asks to move it. A request to plan flexible or multi-day work can include proposed appointment times. Keep existing commitments unless their revision is requested. Evaluate a combined proposal across shared team members, selected tasks and other competing work so one person's proposed work is not double-booked.

For an explicitly supplied customer guide, companion practice or other reusable rule source, establish its identity, available version or observation time, and applicability to the requested account, dataset and planning period. If the source is unavailable, mismatched or its applicability is unresolved, disclose the limitation without substituting example-pattern defaults. Retain independently established choices that still apply, distinguishing them from unverified source content. Follow the current user's direct choices and clarify a material conflict only when it remains unresolved. An ordinary instruction such as “use a 15-minute buffer” needs no formal source document, version or policy interview.

For a revision, retain the prior task and resource selections, appointments, constraints and adopted choices within their applicable scope unless the user changes them. Keep reported unavailable intervals with their source and scope. Switching accounts or synthetic datasets does not automatically transfer customer practices or exclusions. “Also exclude” adds to earlier exclusions; “only” replaces the specified selection. Explain requested changes separately from new account evidence. Use current task, resource and availability evidence for the revised proposal; do not silently switch a previously adopted example default when that example changes. A proposal remains read-only. Do not store one-time choices or edit a companion automatically.

Give every selected task a disposition: proposed with its interval and participants, conflicting, unresolved with the material gap, or otherwise outside the supported proposal. State relevant source scope, observation time, tradeoffs, links and gaps near the result. Distinguish a recorded fact, skill match, conditional suggestion, supported availability and verified change by what the evidence establishes; do not require a fixed answer format. Do not claim an optimum, maximum assignment count or real-world infeasibility from an incomplete search. Consult [scheduling patterns](scheduling-patterns.md) when helpful; apply their restrictive or numeric rules only when explicitly selected or established customer practice. For broad customer practices, point to [tailoring the FSM skill](tailor-fsm-skill.md) only when the user asks for a reusable skill; do not turn an ordinary scheduling request into a tailoring interview.

## Apply an authorized scheduling change

Resolve the exact CRM Task or Project Task, resource, intended primary or team change, reserved timed interval, duration, IANA timezone, and whether the request covers one occurrence or a series. “Assign” and “add to team” have different effects. Clarify only a material ambiguity about the requested change. Read the current assignment, team, status, type, case, customer, asset, booking, Due Date, recurrence, native and FSM schedule representations, and relevant actual timestamps. Inspect task skills and resource qualifications where the account uses them. For series or generated work, follow [recurrence and programs](../references/recurrence-and-programs.md). A single-task request does not imply a series or program change.

Establish the account's effective availability sources, which it maintains and whether it treats each as informative or required. Check the adopted sources for affected resources and the requested interval, including relevant primary and team commitments, Project Tasks, resource allocations, calendars, holidays, absences and continuing work. Include leave requests only when the account exposes and uses them; their status determines whether they block time. Compare instants across timezones and daylight-saving boundaries. Check the physical site separately from the appointment timezone. If the address and stored FSM or asset timezone disagree, keep the task in the proposal and resolve the intended appointment timezone before changing it; continue resolved items. Follow [Check availability and fit](#check-availability-and-fit) for the relevant itinerary, travel and breaks. Resolve material feasibility gaps before the affected write; conditional proposals and independently resolved work may continue. Travel and breaks are schedule gaps, not task records.

Use a supported write path for the requested operation. Verify CRM or Project Task eligibility, booking, subsidiary scope, edit mode and native Project Task prerequisites when relevant. Keep Project Task resource assignments and native constraints separate from CRM Task fields. Keep native and FSM schedule synchronization in view: a native-only edit may be overwritten, and a generic record update need not reproduce Schedule Board Save. Do not copy field-clearing tricks to emulate it. Account for configured notifications and native assignment email separately; saving does not prove delivery. Preserve the current team, booking, task type, status, skill requirements, actual timestamps, unrelated assignments and settings unless the user requested their change. Do not reset status, change Constraint Type or enable Project resources merely to make the change possible.

Reread the affected records before writing. After the supported change, verify persisted primary and team assignments, interval, timezone, native and FSM dates, recurrence scope and relevant downstream processing state. Check the resulting itinerary and gaps against the adopted constraints. Report what was verified, what remains asynchronous or unobserved, and any specific unsupported operation or unmet prerequisite. Prior authorization for the requested change does not need to be requested again.
