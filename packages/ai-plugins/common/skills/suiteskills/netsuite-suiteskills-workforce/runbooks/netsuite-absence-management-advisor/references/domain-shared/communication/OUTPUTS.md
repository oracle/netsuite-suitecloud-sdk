# Structured Outputs

For Portfolio and Group absence-management insights, default to one row per requested group, time-off type, period, and status. Show affected employees, eligible employees, and rate. A typical or range figure per affected employee may be shown when useful. If negligible source amounts materially affect the count, disclose their count and observed range without filtering them out. Preserve approved and pending planned absence as separate rows or columns. Never merge days with hours, or mix request statuses in a total without clearly labeling the inclusion rule.

For a Portfolio expiry review, the default department exposure table is an allowed exception to the no-bare-total rule. Group it by entitlement cycle and next applicable event date, with `Time-Off Type` as a column rather than a separate table. Sort by net total hours at risk. Its headers are `Department`, `Time-Off Type`, `Hours at Risk`, a blank continuation header for the text bar, `Affected Employees`, and `Average Hours per Affected Employee`, in that order. Right-align the total in `Hours at Risk` and render the text block bar in the blank continuation column. Keep the affected count and per-person average beside the total so department size is visible. Do not present this ordering as a rate-based concentration finding or add membership-basis or date-source columns. A later carryover-expiry event is disclosed only by date because its future amount depends on usage. A requested team view inserts `Supervisor` immediately after `Department`. Include a column only when its values differ between rows. An individual expiry list has exactly `Employee`, `Department`, `Supervisor`, and `Hours at Risk`; state the shared event and date in its heading, not repeated columns. Add `Outcome` only for a mixed-mechanism list where it varies. The bar is reserved for the department table.

For a current-liability request, show the Time-Off Balance Summary report's `Balance` totals by entitlement-cycle run and time-off type, in hours. Do not calculate a substitute total from Time-Off Changes or combine it with future hours at risk.

Minimum Portfolio/Group overview columns: grouping attribute, time period, time-off type/plan label, affected employees, eligible employees, affected rate, source effective/as-of basis, retrieval timestamp. The default portfolio expiry department table follows its required six-column physical shape, including `Time-Off Type` and the blank bar-continuation column, instead of this general overview.

Minimum planned-absence columns: supervisor team, optional department context, requested date window, unique employees absent, eligible employees, remaining employees when a named group is in scope, exact status, retrieval timestamp. Approved rows use `USAGE` applied dates; pending rows use requested dates.

For Individual mode, include signed hours and transaction detail needed to audit the answer. Order multi-employee expiry drill-downs by hours at risk descending within the presentation-priority type grouping; do not round a nonzero amount away or place it with fully carried-forward rows. In expiry narrative, lead with the balance immediately before the event and the resulting disposition rather than a mechanical post-expiry zero. Describe the expiry and next-day carryover as one net mechanism, not separate findings; if an accessible carryover cap limits the amount restored, state the cap value and its causal role. Add request and record IDs only when individual-level follow-up is necessary, authorized, and requested; never add employee or supervisor internal IDs beside their names. Use they/them for every employee. Do not put unsupported staffing-risk, attendance, or performance labels in an export.

Any table of Time-Off Changes carries a `Running Balance` column after each transaction, regardless of workflow. An individual expiry answer may use event arithmetic only — pre-event balance, expiry, next-day carryover, and net outcome — and omit transaction history. Do not show a sequence of Time-Off Changes without the running balance that makes it auditable.
