# Group Concentration Analysis

Use this method for Portfolio and Group analysis of expiry exposure or idle/low-use exposure. Planned-absence availability follows its dedicated supervisor-overlap method.

## Ranking

For each eligible department and every direct-supervisor group with at least four members, calculate the affected-employee rate from employees with nonzero net expiry or applicable idle-balance exposure. Compare every group with the same population's company-wide rate before applying attribution; a supervisor team that exceeds the population rate is a finding even if its department is at that rate because other teams offset it. Rank only groups that exceed that rate; always show affected and eligible counts beside the percentage. Average net hours for affected employees may support an individual drill-down but never determine the ranking. Do not treat supervisor analysis as merely an offered drill-down.

When all eligible group rates are materially similar to the company rate, report that nothing stands out instead of returning an arbitrary top-three list.

In a default Portfolio answer, state each genuine supervisor-team outlier in one concise finding line with the team name, affected and eligible counts, and population rate. Do not print a full supervisor breakdown table unless requested; offer it as the drill-down.

## Type-level interpretation

Assess group concentration separately for each time-off type. A near-universal expiry outcome for a type with no carryover is a plan characteristic, not a concentration located in a department or supervisor group. Exclude that type from low-use signals and group rankings, state why it was excluded, and analyze types whose outcomes actually vary between employees.

For a question spanning multiple types, present concentration findings by type rather than pooling their affected rates. This prevents a universal plan outcome from obscuring a type whose employee outcomes vary. When a workflow requires a combined unique-employee low-use count, omit excluded near-universal types; the combined result may be a descriptive headline, but it must not be used to rank groups or replace the per-type concentration findings.

## Attribution

Evaluate each dimension independently, then report one finding at the level that explains the concentration:

1. Start with the broadest elevated candidate and remove each qualifying subgroup in turn.
2. If the remainder returns to the company rate, the subgroup explains the broader result; report the subgroup.
3. If the remainder remains elevated, report the broader group.
4. Job title and sub-tree are not grouping dimensions. If a direct-supervisor team isolates the elevated department result, report the team; otherwise report the department and state that it did not isolate to one team.
5. State the decision in one source-supported line, including the dimension and membership basis.

Do not apply this removal test to a subgroup with fewer than four eligible employees. Department and direct-supervisor populations are different views; never silently combine them.
